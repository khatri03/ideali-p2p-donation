A complete walkthrough of the donation module as it exists in the codebase today — how an organizer builds a campaign in nine steps, how a donor gives money in two steps, how the money moves through Stripe, and what happens afterwards. Written to be read by an engineer, a product owner, or an executive without a translation layer in between.

Section 1

What the module does

The whole module in one paragraph, then the parts.

Read this first

An organizer (a nonprofit or a charity on the platform) builds a campaign — a public fundraising page with a goal, a story, a picture and a set of suggested amounts. The platform gives them a public link, a QR code and an embeddable widget. A donor opens that link, picks an amount, enters their contact details, pays by card or bank, and receives a receipt by email. Everything after that — invoices, donor records, recurring charges, exports, CRM sync — is bookkeeping around those two events.

Build

Organizer creates a campaign through a 9-step wizard with a live phone/desktop preview beside it.

Each step saves to its own backend endpoint, so a half-finished campaign survives a browser close.

Collect

Publishing produces a public page at /donate/:campaignId that needs no login, plus a QR code and an iframe/popup embed.

Bot protection sits in front of the submit, Stripe handles the card data.

Operate

Invoices, donor lists, recurring subscriptions, archives, exports to Excel/PDF/CSV, and a push of donors into a connected marketing CRM.

Donors get their own portal to see history and cancel recurring gifts.

The one thing to remember: this frontend never touches card numbers. It asks the backend to create a Stripe payment intent, hands the sensitive fields to Stripe's own components, and posts only a Stripe token reference back to our API. That design is what keeps PCI scope narrow.

Section 2

Architecture and code map

Where every piece lives, and which layer is allowed to talk to which.

Layer rules
  1. Screen components render and hold local state. They never call axios directly.
  2. Services (app/service/organizer/donation/*) own every HTTP call and are pure TypeScript — no React, no JSX.
  3. HttpClient is the single axios instance. It attaches the bearer token, sends credentials, and retries once after refreshing an expired token.
  4. Redux slice (store/slices/donationSlice.ts) holds the campaign list, the wizard form and the campaign being donated to. Everything else is component state.
Request path
  • Component
    fires an effect or a handler, never a fetch inside JSX
  • Redux thunk or direct service call
    thunks for shared state, direct calls for screen-local data
  • Service function
    builds the URL and query string, types the response
  • HttpClient → .NET API
    auth header, 401 refresh queue, withCredentials

Section 4

Creating a campaign — the nine-step wizard

What each step asks for, what it validates, and where it saves.

How saving works — this is the important bit

The wizard is not one big form submitted at the end. Step 1 creates the campaign record and returns a uniqueId; every later step posts only its own slice of data to its own endpoint with a ?stepNo=N marker. The backend keeps a list of completed steps, which the UI re-reads on load. That is why an organizer can leave halfway, come back days later, and land exactly where they stopped — and why Save & Exit works on every step.

1

Basic Information

Campaign name, start date, end date.

Validates
Name required. Both dates required and must parse. End date must be strictly after start date, enforced with both an inline error and a toast.
Saves to
POST /api/donation/campaign/create on first save; .../set-basic-info on every edit after that.
Side effect
The returned uniqueId is stored in Redux and every later step depends on it.
2

Set Goal

Target amount, plus a switch for whether donors see the goal at all.

Why the switch matters
A campaign can raise money without publishing a target. When it is off, the public page hides the progress thermometer entirely.
Saves to
POST /api/donation/campaign/{id}/set-goal?stepNo=2
3

Add Description

The campaign story, written in a rich-text editor (TipTap) with bold, lists, headings, alignment, links, images and special characters.

AI assist
An inline panel takes a plain-language prompt, calls POST /api/OpenAI/ask, converts the returned markdown to editor HTML, and offers insert or replace. The writer stays in control — nothing is auto-applied.
Saves to
POST .../set-description?stepNo=3
4

Payment Account

Pick which connected merchant account receives the money, then tick which payment methods that account offers.

Dependency
Accounts come from /api/organizer/payment-account/items; methods are fetched per account. No account connected means no way to publish a working campaign.

POST .../set-payment-account?stepNo=4

Each preset carries
An amount and a short impact line, e.g. "Can help feeding 30 children". That description is what turns a number into a reason to give.
Consequence
Which blocks are switched on decides which frequency tabs the donor sees. All three off means the donor only gets a free-text amount box.

Where it lands

Banner Image

Three ways to get a picture, all landing in the same crop editor with a zoom slider.

Review & Publish

Everything from steps 1–8 read back from /review-data, then one irreversible button.

Publish
POST .../publish. Guarded against double-submit and against re-publishing an already-published campaign.
Then
Status flips to Published, confetti runs for four seconds, and a success modal hands over the live donation link.
Live preview

The wizard renders the donor-facing page beside the form and updates it as the organizer types — name, description, goal, colour, banner and presets all reflect immediately.

Two modes: phone frame and desktop frame, toggled from the header. On screens below the md breakpoint the preview is hidden by default and opens as a slide-over.

Navigation controls on every step
  • Back One Step — no save, just move.
  • Skip — steps 5, 7 and 8 only. Optional content stays optional.
  • Save & Next — validates, posts that step, marks it complete, advances.
  • Save & Exit — posts the current step, then leaves. The step rail lets the organizer jump straight back to any step that already holds data.
  • Exit — a confirmation dialog first, so a stray click never discards work silently.
Section 5

Getting the campaign in front of donors

Four distribution channels ship today, all from one Share modal.

Direct link

A copy-to-clipboard field with the public URL /donate/{campaignId}, meant for email and SMS.

QR code

Generated server-side from the donation link, downloadable as PNG for posters, screens and printed appeals. Behind ViewQrCode.

Embed

Two snippets: an inline iframe that sits in a page, and a popup script that opens the form over the organizer's own site. Behind ViewEmbeddedCode.

Social

Facebook share and Messenger send, routed through /api/social-sharing/share/facebook/{id} so the link carries proper preview metadata.

Sharing is gated on status. Share, donor list, recurring and the Donate button are all disabled while a campaign is a Draft, and the disabled controls show a not-allowed cursor rather than failing silently on click.

Section 6

The donation flow — what a donor actually does

Public, no account required, two steps.

Step 1 — Choose the gift
  • Frequency tabs — One Time, Monthly, Yearly. Only the frequencies the organizer switched on in wizard step 5 appear.
  • Preset buttons — the configured amounts, each showing its impact line underneath.
  • Custom amount — a free-text field capped at five digits, rejecting zero and negatives inline.
  • Confirmation line — "You're donating $50 monthly", so the choice is never ambiguous.

The page also shows the banner, the story, and — if the organizer allowed it — the goal with progress against it.

Step 2 — Pay
  • Contact details — first and last name, email, phone. Each has its own validator with a field-level message.
  • Optional note — up to 1,000 characters, in memory of / in honour of, and similar.
  • Payment method — only the methods the campaign's payment account actually supports.
  • Platform tip — see below.
  • Bot check — Cloudflare Turnstile. No token, no submit.

A confirmation dialog stands between the button and the charge; wallet payments skip it because the wallet sheet is itself the confirmation.

The tip model — how the platform funds itself

Plain English

The donor is asked to add a small optional amount on top of their gift, described as "help keep our platform free for charities". Percentage options come from the API (/api/donation/preset-tips), one of which is marked default and is pre-selected. The donor can pick a different percentage or type any amount, including zero. The charity receives the full donation either way — the tip is a separate line, added to the total, and travels to the API as tipDetail with its own description such as Tip Amount-15%.

Why this matters commercially: it is the same revenue model Zeffy uses — zero platform fee to the nonprofit, funded by voluntary donor contributions. It is the reason the pricing page can say "free" without an asterisk, and it is already wired end to end in this codebase.

Payment methods supported

Method Region What the donor sees How it is processed
Credit / debit card Any Card fields rendered by Stripe, plus a cardholder-name box of our own. Stripe Elements creates a payment method; only its ID reaches our API.
Google Pay / Apple Pay Any supported device A native wallet sheet. Shown only when the device and browser support it. Stripe Payment Request. The sheet must open synchronously from the click, so all validation runs before it.
ACH bank debit United States Routing number (9 digits), account number, account and holder type. Backend creates the intent, Stripe collects and verifies the bank account, then the payment is confirmed explicitly.
PAD bank debit Canada Institution number (3 digits), transit number (5 digits), account number. Same three-stage pattern as ACH, on the Canadian rails.
Cheque Any Cheque number and an optional description. Recorded offline — no card network involved. The invoice is created for reconciliation.

End to end, in order

  • Page loads
    Redux fetches the campaign from /api/donation/{id}/donate — story, colour, banner, presets, allowed payment methods, payment account. The banner is pulled as a blob and shown behind a skeleton.
  • Stripe credentials fetched
    The campaign's payment account returns a publishable key and a connected-account ID. Stripe is initialised for that specific charity, so funds route to their account, not a platform pool.
  • Donor picks amount and frequency
    Blocked from continuing on a zero, blank or over-length amount.
  • Donor fills contact, note, tip and payment details
    Turnstile renders and issues a token in the background.
  • Submit — validation gate
    Amount, method, names, email, phone, Turnstile token, and every method-specific rule (card fields complete, 9-digit routing, 3-digit institution, cheque number present) must pass before anything is charged.
  • Payment authorisation
    Card: Stripe creates a payment method. Wallet: the native sheet confirms. ACH/PAD: intent → collect → confirm. Cheque: nothing to authorise.
  • Donation posted
    POST /api/donation/{campaignId}/donate with the amount, frequency, method, note, tip, contact and the Stripe references — carrying the Turnstile token in a request header.
  • Outcome shown
    Success modal, or a failure modal carrying the API's message. The receipt email defined in wizard step 8 goes out, and an invoice record is created.

What is posted to our API on a card gift — note what is absent: no card number, no CVV, no expiry.

{ donationAmount, frequency, paymentMethod, Notes?, paymentMethodDetail: { paymentMethodId, paymentIntentId?, cardHolderName? }, contact: { firstName, middleName?, lastName, primaryEmail, cellPhone? }, tipDetail: { description?, amount? } }

Section 7

Payment architecture

How money reaches the charity rather than the platform.

Stripe Connect, per organizer

Each organizer connects their own Stripe account through an OAuth handshake:

  1. The platform reads its OAuth settings and sends the organizer to Stripe.
  2. Stripe returns an authorisation code.
  3. The backend exchanges that code for the connected account ID and publishable key.
  4. Campaigns then reference that payment account, and payments are initialised against it.

A cache keyed on publishable key + connected account prevents re-initialising Stripe on every render while keeping two charities' contexts strictly separate.

Where the sensitive data goes
  • Card number, expiry and CVV are typed into Stripe's own iframe fields. Our React code cannot read them.
  • Bank details for ACH/PAD are passed directly to Stripe's collection call.
  • Our API receives references only — a payment method ID and a payment intent ID.

Card brand and last-four come back later from the API for display in invoices and recurring records.

Bank debit (ACH / PAD), stage by stage
  • Create the intent
    Our backend calls Stripe with the amount, the tip as an admin fee, the donor's name and email, the bank details, and metadata tying the payment to this campaign and the Donation module. It returns a client secret.
  • Collect the bank account
    Stripe opens its own modal and validates the account against the client secret. Any error here surfaces as a clear message and the flow stops.
  • Confirm
    The payment is explicitly confirmed with the collected payment method.
  • Record
    Only then is the donation posted to our API, with the payment method and intent IDs attached.

Multi-currency is account-scoped. The merchant list and currency list come from admin reference data, and a payment account is bound to one currency. A campaign therefore collects in the currency of the account chosen in wizard step 4 — not in the donor's local currency.

Section 8

After the gift — operations

Invoices, donors, recurring gifts, archives, exports and CRM sync.

Invoices

Every donation produces an invoice. Lists exist both platform-wide and per campaign, filterable by status, free-text search, and member vs non-member.

Statuses: Pending Payment Partially Paid Paid Cancelled Refund Adjusted In System

Labels and badge colours come from one shared map, so a status never renders two different ways in two screens. Invoices can be re-sent by email, downloaded as PDF, and opened on a public link.

Donors

A donor record joins campaign, invoice and contact — name, email, phone, address, gift amount and date. Available across all campaigns or scoped to one.

Selecting donors enables a manual push into a connected marketing list; leaving the selection empty configures an ongoing automatic sync instead.

Recurring donations

Monthly and yearly gifts become subscriptions with their own list, filterable by state: Processing Waiting Failed Cancelled

Each row shows the donor, the amount, the card brand and last four, the last charge date and the next one. Cancelling is confirmed in a modal, and an already-cancelled row offers no cancel action at all.

CRM / marketing sync

The Auto Sync modal lists the organizer's connected integrations, loads the audiences or lists inside the chosen one, and can create a new list without leaving the screen.

Two modes: queue the selected donors once, or save a configuration so future donors flow automatically.

Exports

Campaign list, invoice list, donor list, recurring list and archive list each export to the format the user picks. The file streams as a blob and the filename is taken from the response header when the server supplies one.

A confirmation dialog precedes the download so a large export is never triggered by accident.

Archive, duplicate, delete

Duplicate clones a campaign as a fresh draft — the fastest route to next year's appeal.

Archive moves a Draft or Ended campaign out of the main grid into its own list.

Delete is destructive and sits behind a confirmation modal.

Campaign grid — the organizer's home screen

A card per campaign showing the banner, the status badge, the goal and how much of it has been raised, and the invoice count. Search runs against the API; date filters offer Today, This Week, This Month and a custom range.

Every card carries a three-dot menu whose items enable and disable by status — the same rule set described in the lifecycle table above.

Section 9

The donor's own portal

What a logged-in member sees about their own giving.

Giving history

A paged, searchable table of every gift, each row opening a detail modal with the full record.

Receipts

A download button per gift pulls the invoice PDF — the artefact donors need at tax time.

Recurring control

Active subscriptions with a self-service cancel, so a donor never has to email the charity to stop a monthly gift.

Access is module-gated: the My Donations navigation item only appears when the signed-in user's allowed-modules claim includes Donation, so a membership-only organisation never sees a donation menu.

Section 11

State management

What lives in Redux
  • The campaign list with its paging, search and date filters.
  • The wizard form, the current step and the set of completed steps.
  • The campaign currently being donated to.
  • Per-area loading flags and per-area error strings — list, detail, create, update, donation, banners.
  • A short-lived response cache keyed by query, with explicit invalidation.
Async thunks

fetchList · fetchById · fetchForEdit · create · updateBasicInfo · fetchForDonation · submit · fetchBanners · fetchCompletedSteps

Resuming an edit fans out eight step reads in parallel and merges them into one form object, which is why re-opening a half-built campaign feels instant rather than sequential.

Cache caveat: the list cache is time-based and has no cross-tab invalidation. After a mutation the code refetches explicitly. Any new mutation must remember to do the same, or the grid will show stale numbers until the entry expires.

Section 12

Known gaps and risks

Measured against this repository's own production quality bar. Stated plainly rather than softened.

G1 Debug output in shipped code

279 console.* calls across the donation components, services and slice — including full donation payloads printed with JSON.stringify, and Stripe client secrets logged during the ACH flow.

Risk: donor names, emails and payment references are written to the browser console of every visitor. Fix: strip them; where a log is genuinely needed, gate it behind import.meta.env.DEV.

G2 Untyped boundaries

115 : any annotations in this module, several of them on service return types and on the donation payload itself.

Risk: a backend contract change compiles cleanly and fails at runtime, in a payment path. Fix: declare the shapes in app/interface/donationInter and remove any from every service signature.

G3 Responsive debt

The module uses responsive props in 103 places, but 9 grids are still hard-coded to a fixed column count — including the donation preset buttons (columns={2}) and the payment-method picker (repeat(2, 1fr)) — and 100vh is used in 15 places instead of 100dvh.

Risk: cramped preset buttons at 320–375 px, and a checkout whose bottom is cut off by mobile browser chrome. Fix: responsive column objects, and minH="100dvh".

G4 Oversized components

createDonation.tsx is 1,968 lines and donateToCampaign.tsx is 1,717 — against a repo target of roughly 150 lines per page component. Both fetch, validate, orchestrate payment and render.

Risk: the payment orchestration is untestable in isolation and hard to review. Fix: lift each payment method into its own hook and each wizard step handler into its own module.

G5 Hardcoded values

The Turnstile site key is a literal in the checkout file, and getBannerImageUrl hardcodes a testing host (api.testing.Ideali.com) rather than reading the configured base URL.

Note: a Turnstile site key is public by design, so this is a configuration defect, not a leaked secret. The hardcoded host is a live bug for any environment that is not testing. Fix: move both into validated build-time configuration.

G6 Zero test coverage

The repository has no test runner and no test files. Nothing in this module — not the amount validation, not the ACH sequence, not the tip calculation — is covered by an automated check.

Risk: every change to a payment path is verified by hand or not at all. Fix: this is an approved-dependency decision, not a drive-by; it needs a deliberate call.

G7 Money handled as floats

Amounts and tips are parsed with parseFloat and added directly; the total is a string produced by toFixed(2).

Risk: binary floating point rounding on a currency total. Fix: do the arithmetic in minor units (integer cents) and format only at the edge, with Intl.NumberFormat.

G8 Silent failures and defensive guessing

Several wizard read calls swallow their error and return null, so a failed load looks identical to an empty field. Field extraction guesses across three or four possible response key names.

Risk: an organizer sees a blank step, saves it, and overwrites real data with empty values. Fix: surface a retryable error, and pin one contract per endpoint.

G9 Hard caps with no explanation

The custom donation amount is capped at five digits, so the largest gift a donor can type is 99,999. There is no message telling them why the field stops accepting input.

Risk: a major donor is silently blocked at the checkout. Fix: make the limit configurable and state it in the UI.

G10 No peer-to-peer capability

The module is a direct-giving product: organizer creates a campaign, donors give to it. There is no individual fundraiser page, no team, no team captain, no leaderboard, no supporter-side sharing or milestone email.

Context: those are exactly the items the competitive spec names as the disqualifying v1 floor for peer-to-peer. Everything below is what the existing module already gives that build a head start.

What is already in place that a peer-to-peer build can reuse. This is a strong foundation, not a blank page:

  • A public, login-free donation page with a themeable identity and a working goal thermometer.
  • Stripe Connect per organizer, with card, wallet, ACH, PAD and cheque already wired.
  • The tip-funded revenue model, live and configurable from the API.
  • Recurring gifts, invoices, receipts and a donor self-service portal.
  • Share, QR and embed distribution, plus Facebook sharing with server-rendered preview metadata.
  • A CRM sync path for pushing donors into a marketing list.

Reference

Glossary

Organizer
The nonprofit or charity account that owns campaigns and receives the money.
Campaign
One fundraising effort with a goal, a story, a banner, dates and suggested amounts.
Draft
A campaign that has been started in the wizard but never published. Not reachable by donors.
Preset amount
A suggested giving amount with an impact line attached, e.g. $50 — "feeds 30 children".
Frequency
One Time, Monthly or Yearly. Monthly and Yearly create a recurring subscription.
Goal thermometer
The progress bar comparing money raised to the target. Hidden if the organizer switched off goal visibility.
Platform tip
The optional extra the donor adds on top of their gift to fund the platform. Never deducted from the charity's amount.
Payment account
The organizer's connected merchant account. Determines the currency and which payment methods a campaign can offer.
Stripe Connect
Stripe's model for paying an organisation other than the platform. Each organizer authorises the platform to charge on their behalf.
Payment intent
Stripe's record of an attempted payment. Created before money moves and confirmed once the donor authorises.
ACH / PAD
Direct bank debit in the United States (ACH) and Canada (PAD). Cheaper than cards for large gifts, slower to clear.
Turnstile
Cloudflare's bot check. Produces a token the backend verifies before accepting a donation.
Invoice
The financial record of a gift, and the basis of the donor's receipt.
Recurring subscription
A monthly or yearly gift that charges automatically until it is cancelled or fails.
Auto sync
Pushing donor contact records into a connected marketing list, either once or continuously.

In one sentence: the donation module is a complete, production-wired direct-giving product — nine-step campaign builder, two-step public checkout, five payment method families on Stripe Connect, and full post-gift operations — carrying real quality debt in logging, typing, component size and money arithmetic, and missing only the social layer that would make it peer-to-peer.
