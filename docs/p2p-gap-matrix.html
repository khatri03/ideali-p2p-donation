1 · How to read this document

Three sources, one table.

Market column

What competitors offer, drawn from the 12-platform competitive spec. These are vendor claims as captured in that research — not independently re-verified here.

ideali.io column

What the donation module actually does, read directly from the frontend source. This column is evidence-based, not aspirational.

Verdict column

The call, plus which requirement (R1–R6) or release phase it belongs to, so a row can go straight into the backlog.

The four verdicts

  • Have  Working in production today. No build needed for parity.
  • Partial  Exists but incomplete, limited in scope, or lives in another module — needs work before it counts.
  • Missing  No implementation. Must be built.
  • Unverified  May exist in the backend API; the frontend shows no evidence either way. Confirm before scheduling.

Scope of the evidence. The ideali.io column reflects the frontend repository only. The backend is a separate service. Where a capability could plausibly exist server-side without any UI, it is marked Unverified rather than Missing — those rows need a backend check before anyone commits an estimate.

2 · The scorecard at a glance

Where the 56 capabilities land.

Payments and campaign building are strong

Nearly every "have" sits in two clusters: taking money and building a campaign page. These are the expensive, compliance-heavy parts of a fundraising platform, and they are done.

The gap is one shaped hole, not scatter

Twelve of the 24 missing capabilities are a single feature family: the fundraiser and team layer. They are missing together because none of them exists without the others.

Automation is the second cluster

Milestone emails, stalled-progress nudges and an AI coach are how competitors keep fundraisers active. We send transactional email only.

The headline. ideali.io has built the hard half. What remains is a layer that sits on top of the existing campaign — it does not replace or rework the donation engine that already runs.

4 · The core gap, explained plainly

Twelve missing rows are really one missing idea.

What we have today

A campaign is a page the charity owns. The charity writes the story, sets the goal, shares the link, and donors give. One page, one goal, one story, one audience — the charity's own.

This is crowdfunding. It works, and it is finished.

What peer-to-peer adds

Supporters raise on behalf of the charity. Sara joins the campaign, gets her own page with her own photo, her own reason and her own goal, and asks her own network. Her friends give to her — the money reaches the charity, the credit reaches Sara, and her total rolls up into her team and the campaign.

One campaign becomes a hundred pages and a hundred networks. That multiplication is the entire value of the model.

Why the twelve rows cannot be split up

A fundraiser page with no attribution raises money that lands nowhere. Attribution with no leaderboard removes the reason people compete. A leaderboard with no team layer flattens the social structure that recruits new fundraisers. Each piece is load-bearing for the others.

This is why the competitive research calls their absence disqualifying rather than merely a weakness: a buyer comparing us against Givebutter or Bloomerang would not score us lower on P2P — they would score us as not having it.

The honest position. ideali.io today is a capable donation platform. It is not a peer-to-peer platform, and no combination of existing screens adds up to one. The fundraiser layer must be built.

5 · Where we already lead or match

The gap analysis is only useful next to the credit column.

Ahead Payment breadth

Card, wallets, US bank debit, Canadian bank debit and cheque, with per-charity payouts. Several mid-market competitors do not offer bank debit at all.

Ahead AI inside the editor

Story drafting, plus AI and stock imagery. Fundraise Up launched AI assistance in 2026 and DonorDrive still has none — we are early, not late, on the R4 vector.

Ahead Tip-based revenue model

The donor-covered model that made Zeffy and Givebutter the goodwill leaders is already live here — the mechanism R6 recommends is not a build, it is a pricing decision.

Match Campaign builder depth

A nine-step guided builder with resumable saves and live preview is at or above the mid-market standard.

Match Back office

Invoices with partial payments, recurring management, donor self-service cancellation and three export formats.

Foundation CRM sync

Contact sync already runs. R2 asks us to make CRM-native data flow the flagship differentiator — the pipe exists, it needs fundraiser attribution flowing through it.

6 · What to build, in order

The missing rows, sequenced against the R1–R6 recommendations.

v1 — the floor

Without all of it, we cannot say we have P2P
  • Fundraiser entity and public fundraiser page
  • Team entity, team page, captain role
  • Participant self-signup and join-a-team flow
  • Participant dashboard — my total, my donors, my share tools
  • Goals and thermometers at fundraiser and team level
  • Page customisation: photo, story, personal thank-you
  • Gift attribution and roll-up to team and campaign
  • Leaderboards weighted by dollars and donor count
  • Automated milestone and stalled-progress emails
  • Fundraiser and donor records reaching the CRM with soft credit

v1.5 — credibility

What buyers ask about in the second meeting
  • "Register to fundraise" campaign type R5
  • Early-bird and date-based registration pricing
  • AI coach — story drafting and nudges for fundraisers R4
  • Fundraiser toolkit: ready-made ask emails and social copy
  • Badges, contests and incentives
  • Public supporter wall and countdown widgets
  • Custom questions on the donation form

v2 and later

Competitive extras, not entry requirements
  • Facebook fundraiser sync and in-feed donate R4
  • Donor-facing multi-currency
  • Matching gifts
  • Text-to-give
  • Crypto, stock and DAF gifts

Native mobile app and livestreaming stay out of scope while the target is the mid-market. Revisit only if the base turns out to be enterprise endurance-event organisations.

Sequencing logic. R1 puts us in the mid-market, where Bloomerang, Funraise and GiveSmart compete. Every v1 item above is something all three already ship. Nothing in v1 is differentiation — it is the price of being compared at all. Differentiation lives in v1.5 and in the CRM data flow that R2 calls the wedge.

7 · What the build gets for free

The v1 list is long, but it starts from a running platform, not a blank page.

Reusable as-is

  • The entire checkout — a gift to a fundraiser is the same transaction with one extra reference
  • All five payment methods, wallets and bank debit included
  • Per-charity payout accounts
  • The tip model and its revenue mechanics
  • Recurring giving and its management screens
  • Receipts, invoices and exports
  • Bot protection on public forms
  • Share links, QR codes and embeds

Reusable with extension

  • The campaign builder — a fundraiser page is a much shorter version of the same idea
  • The AI writing engine — repoint it at fundraiser stories to become the AI coach
  • The image pipeline — stock, AI and crop, reused for fundraiser photos
  • The email template system — extend from thank-you into milestone and nudge templates
  • The progress bar component — reuse at fundraiser and team level
  • The CRM sync pipe — add fundraiser identity and soft credit to what it already sends

What this means for estimates. The heavy, regulated, slow-to-build parts — money movement, payouts, compliance posture, receipting — are done. The v1 work is mostly new entities, new pages and new roll-up logic on top of infrastructure that already carries real transactions.

8 · Risks to price in before committing dates

Four things that will slow the build if they are ignored.

Risk No automated test coverage

There is no test runner in this repository and no tests exist. Adding a fundraiser layer that touches money attribution without tests is how silent miscrediting ships. Test infrastructure should land before, not after, the P2P work.

Risk Debug output in the donation path

The donation module writes extensive debug output in the browser, including donation payloads and payment references. This must be removed before any new public-facing surface multiplies the exposure.

Risk Oversized components

The two central donation screens are roughly 1,700 and 2,000 lines against a house target of about 150. Extending them rather than splitting them first will compound the cost of every subsequent change.

Risk Backend scope unknown

Everything above is read from the frontend. How much of the fundraiser, team and attribution model the API can already express is the single largest unknown in any estimate. Confirm it before committing a date.

Recommended first step. A short backend capability review against the v1 list. It converts the two Unverified rows into decisions and turns this matrix into a schedule instead of a scope.

9 · Caveats — read before quoting this externally

  • Competitor claims are vendor-reported. The market column reproduces the competitive research as captured. Pricing that is not public is an outside estimate, and vendor feature claims were not independently retested here.
  • Our column is frontend-only. A capability with no user interface is recorded as missing or unverified even if the backend could support it.
  • Verdicts are capability-level, not quality-level. "Have" means it exists and works, not that it matches the best implementation in the market.
  • The 56 capabilities are a chosen frame. A different cut of the same market would produce a different count. The proportions matter; the exact numbers do not.
  • This is a point-in-time reading. Both the market and our codebase move. Re-check before any external commitment.

Glossary

Peer-to-peer (P2P)
Supporters fundraise on the charity's behalf through their own pages and networks.
Fundraiser page
An individual supporter's own page inside a campaign, with their photo, story and goal.
Team captain
The supporter who creates a team, recruits members and sees the team's combined total.
Soft credit
Recording that a gift was influenced by a fundraiser, without changing who legally gave it.
Roll-up
A gift counting toward the fundraiser, their team and the campaign at the same time.
Thermometer
The progress bar showing amount raised against a goal.
Tip model
The donor optionally covers the platform's cost so the charity keeps the whole gift.
Register to fundraise
One flow that signs a participant up for an event and opens their fundraising page.

One sentence for the board. ideali.io has built the expensive half of a peer-to-peer platform — money movement, payouts, campaign creation and back office — and none of the visible half; the v1 list is the shortest honest path from donation platform to P2P platform.
