Adding peer-to-peer fundraising

A build plan for turning our donation module into a peer-to-peer platform. It says exactly what gets created, where each piece lives, what the backend must provide, and in what order the work ships. Written to be read by anyone — no prior knowledge of the codebase assumed.

1What we are building, in plain words

Today

A charity creates a campaign. That campaign has one public page. Everybody who gives, gives on that page. The charity does all the asking.

This is crowdfunding. We already do it well.

After this work

Supporters can join a campaign. Each one gets their own page, with their own photo, their own reason for caring, and their own goal. They ask their own friends and family.

This is peer-to-peer. The charity stops being the only fundraiser.

The example we will keep using

The charity runs “Clean Water 2026”, goal £50,000.

  • Sara joins the campaign. She gets her own page: her photo, a sentence about why she cares, her own goal of £500.
  • Sara shares her page link on WhatsApp. Her uncle gives £50 on Sara's page.
  • That £50 goes to the charity like any other gift — same checkout, same payout, same receipt.
  • But it also shows on Sara's total (£50 of £500), on her team's total, and on the campaign's total.
  • Sara appears on a leaderboard. She gets an email when she hits half her goal.

That is the whole feature. Everything below exists to make those five lines work reliably.

2What is in this plan, and what is deliberately not

The gap matrix scored 56 capabilities and found twelve missing ones that form a single feature family. Those twelve are the version 1 scope below. Nothing here is “nice to have” — competitors treat every one of them as the minimum, so shipping eleven of twelve still reads as an incomplete product.

In scope — version 1

  1. Turn peer-to-peer on or off per campaign
  2. Fundraiser (a person) with their own public page
  3. Team, with a captain, holding several fundraisers
  4. Self sign-up: anyone can join a campaign or a team
  5. Participant dashboard: my total, my donors, my link
  6. Personal and team goals, each with a progress bar
  7. Page personalisation: photo, story, goal
  8. Attribution — a gift credits fundraiser, team and campaign at once
  9. Leaderboards, ranked by money raised and number of donors
  10. Milestone and stalled-progress emails to fundraisers
  11. Fundraiser and donor records reaching the CRM with soft credit
  12. Share tools: link, QR, social buttons, on every fundraiser page

Next — version 1.5

  • Register to fundraise (event ticket creates a fundraiser page)
  • AI coach that writes each fundraiser's appeal
  • Fundraiser toolkit: ready-made posts and images
  • Badges and contests
  • Supporter wall and countdown timers
  • Custom form questions on the donation checkout

Not in this plan

  • Facebook fundraiser sync and in-feed giving
  • Donor-facing currency switching
  • Matching gifts, text-to-give, crypto and stock giving
  • Native mobile app, livestream fundraising
  • Any change to how money is taken, held or paid out
One rule for the whole build: the payment path is not touched. Cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay, bank debit, cheque, recurring gifts, tips, receipts and payouts stay exactly as they are today. Peer-to-peer adds a reference to the gift; it does not change the gift.

3The one idea everything hangs on: attribution

If only one paragraph of this document is read, make it this one. Every other feature is decoration on top of it.

Attribution means a donation remembers who asked for it.

Today a donation record answers: which campaign, how much, from whom. After this change it also answers: through which fundraiser, and on which team. Two extra optional fields. That is the entire technical heart of peer-to-peer.

The money still belongs to the charity. Only the credit is shared — the industry calls this a soft credit.

How the £50 is counted

Charity bank account+£50 real money
Campaign total+£50
Sara's team total+£50 credit
Sara's personal total+£50 credit

The money is counted once. The credit is counted at three levels. Nothing is double-banked.

Why it must be built first

  • A leaderboard is just attribution, sorted.
  • A personal progress bar is attribution, summed.
  • A milestone email is attribution, watched.
  • A CRM soft credit is attribution, exported.

Build attribution wrong and all four are wrong together. Build it right and the rest becomes ordinary screen work.

Correctness bar for attribution. A gift may never be credited twice, never be silently lost when a fundraiser is removed, and never be moved without an audit trail. Refunds and failed payments must reverse the credit as well as the money. This is money-adjacent logic and is treated as such.

Why totals are not stored. A fundraiser's raised amount is calculated by summing their attributed gifts, not kept in a column. A stored total drifts the first time a refund is processed outside the normal path; a calculated one cannot. If reading becomes slow, the fix is a cached view that can be rebuilt from the gifts — never a hand-maintained number.
Two decisions needed before build starts. First: does a fundraiser need a full user account, or is an email plus a magic link enough? Full accounts are safer, magic links convert far better. Second: are teams allowed to nest (team inside team)? Recommendation: account required, and no nesting in version 1.

The only change to the checkout call

Our existing request type DonationSubmitRequest gains two optional fields. Empty means an ordinary campaign gift, exactly as today.

{
  donationAmount, frequency, paymentMethod,
  paymentMethodDetail, contact, Notes,

  fundraiserUniqueId?: string,   // new
  teamUniqueId?: string          // new
}
Server-side rules the API must enforce. The frontend can send anything, so the API decides: the fundraiser must belong to the named campaign; the fundraiser must be Active; the team must match the fundraiser's team at that moment; a suspended page accepts no gifts. A bad reference is rejected, never quietly dropped — a silently un-credited gift is worse than a failed one, because nobody notices.
Unknown to close first. Our reading of the system is frontend-only. Some of this may already exist server-side. Before any estimate is committed, walk this table with the API team and mark each row exists, partly exists, or new. That single meeting is the highest-value hour in the whole plan.

Public routes extend the existing /donate/:campaignId pattern rather than inventing a new one, so old links keep working and new ones read naturally. Every route is lazy-loaded behind the shared suspense loader, like all current routes.

State handling

No new global store slice. Fundraiser and team data is fetched per page and held in local state, with the same loading / empty / error triple every screen in this repository uses.

The one exception: when a donor arrives via a fundraiser link, the fundraiser reference is carried through checkout in the existing donation slice, so a page refresh mid-payment cannot lose the credit.

No new dependencies

Everything needed is already installed: Chakra for UI, the existing QR service for share codes, the existing image pipeline for photos, the existing rich text editor for stories, the existing table library for lists.

Nothing in this plan requires approval to add a package.

7The four journeys, step by step

Organiser turns it on

  1. Opens the campaign builder, reaches the new peer-to-peer step
  2. Switches it on; chooses individuals, teams, or both
  3. Sets a suggested personal goal and whether new pages need approval
  4. Publishes. The campaign page now shows a Fundraise for us button

Sara joins

  1. Clicks Fundraise for us, signs in or creates an account
  2. Chooses: on my own, start a team, or join a team
  3. Adds a photo, a few sentences, and her goal — the goal is pre-filled
  4. Gets her link and QR code straight away, with share buttons

Her uncle gives

  1. Opens Sara's link, sees her photo, her story, her progress
  2. Presses Donate — the existing checkout opens, with a line saying “Supporting Sara”
  3. Pays exactly as any donor does today. Same methods, same receipt
  4. Sara's bar moves, the team bar moves, the campaign bar moves. Sara is emailed “Your uncle just gave £50”

Sara keeps going

  1. Her dashboard shows raised, goal, donor count, and every supporter
  2. She thanks people from that list
  3. At half her goal, an automatic congratulations email arrives
  4. If nothing comes in for a week, a gentle nudge arrives with her link ready to share
Note the third journey. The donor's experience is the checkout we already ship — the only visible difference is a line naming who they are supporting. That is why the expensive, regulated, slow-to-get-right half of this feature is already finished.

9Delivery — five milestones, in this order

The order is not negotiable. Each milestone is only useful once the one before it is finished, because every later feature reads the data the earlier one writes.

Milestone 1

Attribution works end to end

Build: the two entities, the two donation fields, the campaign switches, and a fundraiser page that can be reached and donated to. No styling polish, no leaderboard.

Done when: a gift made on a fundraiser link appears on that fundraiser's total, that team's total and the campaign total — and a refund reverses all three.

This milestone carries almost all of the technical risk. Nothing after it is conceptually hard.

Milestone 2

People can join and run a page

Build: join flow, team creation, page personalisation with photo and story, participant dashboard, share bar with link and QR.

Done when: a supporter can go from campaign page to a live, personal, shareable fundraising page without any staff involvement.

Milestone 3

It becomes competitive

Build: leaderboards ranked by money and by donor count, team pages with member progress, captain controls, top-fundraiser panel on the campaign page.

Done when: a campaign with fifty fundraisers is readable, ranked and fast on a phone.

Milestone 4

It keeps people going

Build: milestone emails, stalled-progress nudges, new-donation alerts to the fundraiser, and organiser participant management with approve, suspend and export.

Done when: a fundraiser who stops for a week is contacted automatically, and an organiser can take down a bad page in one click.

Milestone 5

The data reaches the CRM

Build: fundraisers and their donors flow into donor records with the soft credit attached, through the sync pipe we already have.

Done when: a charity can see, in the CRM, that this donor came in through this fundraiser — the thing the competitive brief calls our main differentiator.

What ships to customers, and when

Milestones 1 and 2 together are the first thing worth showing anyone. Milestone 3 is the point at which we survive a side-by-side comparison. Milestones 4 and 5 are what make renewals stick.

Suggest a small closed pilot with two or three friendly charities after milestone 3, before general release.

No dates in this document, deliberately. Sizing depends on how much already exists in the backend, and we have not read the backend. Estimates come after the API contract walkthrough in section 5, not before — a number invented now would be a guess wearing a suit.

10How we keep it correct

Test tooling is a prerequisite, not a follow-up

This repository has no test runner and no tests at all. Attribution decides who is credited with money. Building it with no automated tests means a miscredit can ship and stay invisible until a charity complains.

Recommendation: add the test setup as the first task of milestone 1, before the feature code. It is a small, one-time, approved-dependency change and it protects everything that follows.

What gets tested

  • A gift with a fundraiser reference credits exactly three totals, once each
  • A gift with no reference behaves exactly as it does today
  • A refunded gift removes the credit everywhere it was added
  • A gift to a suspended fundraiser is rejected with a clear message
  • Moving a fundraiser between teams does not rewrite past gifts
  • Leaderboard order is correct for ties, by money and by donor count
  • Only the page owner can edit a page; only the captain can remove a member
  • The join flow, page edit and donate flow work by keyboard alone

Screen quality bar

  • Every screen has a designed loading, empty and error state — a fundraiser page with no gifts yet is a common, and important, first impression
  • Mobile first, checked at 375, 768 and 1280 pixels wide. Most fundraiser links are opened on a phone from a messaging app
  • Long lists scroll inside their own container; the page itself never scrolls sideways
  • Colours, spacing and type come from the shared theme, never hand-picked values
  • Touch targets at least 44 pixels; labelled controls; visible focus rings

Security points specific to this feature

  • Fundraiser stories are user-written and appear on public pages — they must be sanitised before rendering, or the page becomes an attack surface
  • Photos are user-uploaded: type, size and dimension limits enforced server-side
  • Ownership is checked on the server for every edit; hiding a button is not access control
  • Public pages must not leak donor email addresses or amounts marked private
  • Self sign-up is open to the internet, so the join endpoint needs the same bot protection the donate endpoint already has
  • Debug output currently written to the browser console in the donation path must be removed before these new public pages multiply how often it is exposed

11Risks, and what we do about each

RiskSeverityResponse
Backend scope is unknown High Walk the section 5 table with the API team before estimating. This is the single largest unknown in the plan
No automated tests exist High Add the test setup as the first task of milestone 1. Attribution is money logic and cannot ship unverified
Attribution errors are silent High Never store totals; always calculate. Reject bad references loudly. Log every credit change with who and when
The donate screen is already very large Medium Split it into smaller parts as the first step of milestone 1, before adding anything to it
Public pages written by the public Medium Sanitise stories, limit uploads, give organisers a one-click suspend, and support approval-before-live
Leaderboards get slow at scale Medium Page them from day one; add a rebuildable cached view only if measurement shows it is needed
Feature is switched on for campaigns that do not want it Low Off by default. Existing campaigns see no change until an organiser opts in
Scope grows mid-build Low The version 1 list in section 2 is fixed. Anything else goes to version 1.5, in writing

Decisions needed before work starts

  1. Does a fundraiser need a full account, or is email plus a magic link acceptable? Recommend: account.
  2. Can teams contain other teams? Recommend: no, not in version 1.
  3. Are personal goals compulsory or optional per campaign? Recommend: optional, with a suggested default.
  4. Do fundraiser pages need approval before going live? Recommend: a per-campaign switch, off by default.
  5. Is the test setup approved as part of milestone 1? Recommend: yes.
The honest summary. The hard, regulated, slow half of peer-to-peer — taking money safely, paying it out, receipting it, protecting it from bots — is already built and running. What is missing is a layer of pages, links and counting on top of it. That is a well-understood body of work with one genuinely delicate part: making sure every pound is credited to exactly the right person, exactly once.

12Words used in this document

Peer-to-peer
Supporters raise money on the charity's behalf, each with their own page.
Fundraiser
A person raising money inside a campaign. Also the name of their public page.
Team
A group of fundraisers whose totals add up together.
Captain
A team member with the extra right to manage the team page and its members.
Attribution
The record of which fundraiser a gift came through.
Soft credit
Credit for a gift given to someone who did not pay it — the person who asked.
Roll-up
Totals adding upward: fundraiser into team, team into campaign.
Leaderboard
Ranked list of fundraisers or teams, by money raised or by number of donors.
Slug
The readable part of a web address, such as sara-k.
Milestone email
An automatic message sent when a fundraiser passes a share of their goal.
