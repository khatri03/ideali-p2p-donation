Peer-to-peer fundraising

Supporters raise money on a charity's behalf, on their own page, under the charity's control.

What it does

A charity switches peer-to-peer fundraising on for a single donation campaign. From that moment a supporter can set up their own fundraising page for that campaign, share its address, and take donations that are credited both to them and to the campaign. The money goes to the charity exactly as it did before; the page only changes who asked for it.

WhoWhat they can do
A supporter Set up a page, write why it matters to them, add a photo and a goal, share the link, watch what it raises, join or start a team, and see where they stand on the leaderboard.
A donor Give through a supporter's page without holding an account, using exactly the payment methods the campaign already offered.
The charity Switch the feature on or off per campaign, require approval before a page goes live, allow or forbid teams, choose who may see the leaderboard, review and moderate every page and team, invite supporters by email, and edit the six emails supporters receive.

What a charity should know before switching it on

The fundraiser menu appears at the supporter's next sign-in

When somebody sets up their first fundraising page, the menu item that takes them to it does not appear immediately. It appears the next time they sign in. This is deliberate for now: the menu is built from the signed-in session, and refreshing that session mid-journey touches the sign-in path every existing supporter already uses. Until that is changed as its own piece of work, the screen that confirms a page has been created gives the supporter the address of their page directly, so they are never left without a way to reach it.

Approval is a setting, not a default

A campaign can require the charity to approve each page before it is public. With approval off, a page is live the moment it is created. With it on, the page is reachable by whoever holds the link but says it is waiting for approval, publishes neither the story nor a total, and takes no money. The charity is notified when a page is waiting.

Switching the feature off does not delete anything

Turning peer-to-peer off stops new pages and new teams. Pages that already exist stay readable, so a link a supporter has already shared does not break. The same is true of teams: the switch stops new ones forming, it does not disband the ones that exist.

The leaderboard has three settings, and one of them refuses everyone

Public shows the standings to anyone with the address. Only my charity shows them to the charity alone and says so on the screen. Hidden switches the board off entirely, including for the charity. A board that is not public is never linked from a public page, so nobody is offered a link that then refuses them.

What counts as money raised

One definition, used by every screen that reports a figure: settled payments only, tips excluded because a tip is platform revenue rather than money for the cause, processing fees not deducted because the donor gave that amount, refunds and chargebacks subtracted so a total can go down, and a recurring donation counted per settled instalment. A team's total is its current members' totals added up, so a member who leaves takes their money out of the team but keeps it on their own page.

Privacy decisions worth stating plainly

  • A donor who asked to stay anonymous is shown as anonymous on a supporter's page, and is left off the leaderboard's biggest-gifts list entirely rather than ranked as "Anonymous".
  • A donor who did not ask to stay anonymous is shown as a first name and a last initial. No surname, email address or postal address reaches a public screen.
  • Pages the charity has not approved, has paused or has turned down never appear on the leaderboard, whatever they have raised.
  • An invitation email can be unsubscribed from, and an address that hard-bounces or complains is suppressed for that charity so it is not written to again.

How this was tested

This corrects an earlier statement in the build plan. That plan was written when this repository had no test runner, and said the frontend would ship without automated tests. That is no longer true, and the release note says what is actually true instead.
  • Frontend unit tests. Vitest on happy-dom with Testing Library, covering the services, the hooks, the pure helpers and the behaviour of every screen this feature added, failure paths as thoroughly as success paths.
  • Backend service tests. xUnit against an isolated in-memory database, covering every peer-to-peer service including the money definition, the authorisation rules and the enumeration answers.
  • End-to-end tests. Playwright against the running application and the real API, at 1280px, 768px and 375px, plus a sweep at 320, 1024, 1440 and 1920 that also checks headings, control names, alternative text, focus rings, contrast and browser storage. Endpoint suites prove each endpoint refuses a caller with no token, a caller from another charity, and a record that is not theirs, with the same sentence each time. Database suites prove the constraints that only exist in the schema.

Known limitations at this release

Each of these is a decision or an untested edge, written down rather than discovered later.
  • The fundraiser menu appears at the next sign-in, as described above.
  • An email address holds one account tied to one charity. Fundraising for two charities with one address is platform-wide work and is not part of this.
  • The invitation email carries no List-Unsubscribe header. The visible unsubscribe link works and is honoured; the header is a change to the shared sender and has not been made.
  • The lifecycle emails have not been rendered in a real Outlook or Gmail client; they have been proved as sanitised, substituted HTML and previewed in the product.
  • The bounce and complaint feed is wired to the mail provider's webhook and has been exercised with synthetic events, not with a real message to a dead mailbox.
  • No screen-reader pass has been run. Names, alternative text, headings, focus rings and contrast are asserted automatically, which is most of the accessibility rule and not all of it.
  • Offline and cash donations, fundraiser-to-fundraiser messaging, matched giving, and fundraiser pages for events and memberships are deliberately out of scope.

The hourly background job peer-to-peer-lifecycle-job sends the six supporter emails. On its first run against an environment that already has fundraising pages, every email those pages are due is claimed without being sent, so switching this on cannot post a backlog to people who have been fundraising for weeks.
